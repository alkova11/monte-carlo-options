Full methodology ↗
01 / THE MODEL

Risk-neutral geometric Brownian motion

Constant continuously compounded rates and dividend yield. Constant annualised volatility. Exact terminal sampling for European options.

ST = S₀ exp[(r − q − σ²/2)T + σ√T Z]

Z is standard normal. The drift r − q is a pricing-measure drift, not a forecast of future market returns. Spot, strike and prices use consistent currency units; T is in years.

02 / UNCERTAINTY

The right unit of independence

Ordinary Monte Carlo uses independent payoffs. Antithetic sampling uses independent averages of each positive/negative shock pair.

SE = s(U) / √n    CI ≈ estimate ± 1.96 SE

Intervals rely on a normal CLT approximation. Successive convergence points share samples. Their bands are pointwise, not a simultaneous guarantee. Rare payoffs can make observed variance misleadingly small.

03 / VARIANCE REDUCTION

A control with a known expectation

The discounted terminal stock is correlated with the option payoff. Subtract its centred deviation to remove noise without changing the expectation.

YCV = Y − β(X − E[X])

X = exp(−rT)ST and E[X] = S₀ exp(−qT). The coefficient β is fitted on an independent pilot: 10% of the total budget, at least 32 draws. The pilot cost is included.

04 / THE EXTENSION

Arithmetic-average Asian options

Exact GBM increments at explicit equal-weight observation dates. No initial-spot observation unless you include date zero. Payment occurs at maturity.

A = (St₁ + ⋯ + Stₘ) / m

A geometric-average payoff provides an analytical control expectation. It is not the arithmetic option price. The research report uses an independent numerical arithmetic reference with its own standard error.

ALL COMPUTATION STAYS LOCAL

Budget & uncertainty

Your sample budget counts payoff evaluations, including the control pilot.

Ordinary: N independent payoffs. Antithetic: floor(N/2) independent pairs, two payoffs per pair. Control: N − pilot production units. A deterministic contract is priced analytically with zero draws used.

Normal intervals can understate uncertainty for rare events. Approximate 95% coverage is a repeated-run property; any particular interval can miss the reference.
